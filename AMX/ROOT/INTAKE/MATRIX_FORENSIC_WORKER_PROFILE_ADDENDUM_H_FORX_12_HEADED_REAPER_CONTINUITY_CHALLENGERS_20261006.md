## 3. CONTINUITY-CHALLENGER METHOD

Each challenger performs four steps.

### A. AFFIRM
State what the preceding triad has actually established.

### B. PARAPHRASE
Restate it in different words without changing meaning.

### C. CHALLENGE
Compare the paraphrase against:
- prior continuity anchors;
- current evidence;
- current packet;
- current next action.

### D. PIN
Persist the material proposition as a **CONTINUITY ANCHOR**.

A continuity anchor contains:
- anchor ID;
- source heads;
- exact proposition;
- evidence reference;
- lifecycle / authority status;
- whether later reasoning may supersede it;
- supersession source if applicable.

---

## 4. CONTRADICTION RULE

If a later head contradicts a continuity anchor, the challenger must determine whether the difference is:

- **RESTATEMENT** — same meaning;
- **REFINEMENT** — narrower / more precise but compatible;
- **AUTHORIZED SUPERSESSION** — later valid source changes the state;
- **MATERIAL CONTRADICTION** — incompatible claims with no valid supersession;
- **UNKNOWN** — insufficient evidence.

A **MATERIAL CONTRADICTION** cannot be averaged away by the other heads.

It creates a continuity break and routes back to the head / triad responsible for the inconsistent transition.

---

## 5. SARCASM BOUNDARY

The sarcastic/paraphrasing style exists only to expose inconsistency.

Allowed:
- dry repetition;
- mild incredulity;
- “So you are saying X, correct?”
- “But you just said Y.”
- obvious-language contradiction checks.

Not allowed:
- ridicule;
- humiliation;
- changing facts for a joke;
- substituting tone for evidence;
- escalating conflict;
- mocking people rather than the contradiction.

The packet output remains professionally usable even when the internal challenger uses a sharper phrasing.

---

## 6. CUMULATIVE CONTINUITY

The challengers do not inspect only the immediately preceding triad.

They receive the **cumulative accepted chain**.

Therefore:
- Head 4 anchors Heads 1–3;
- Head 8 tests Heads 5–7 against both their own output and Head 4 anchors;
- Head 12 tests Heads 9–11 against the entire chain from Heads 1–11.

This prevents local correctness from masking global contradiction.
