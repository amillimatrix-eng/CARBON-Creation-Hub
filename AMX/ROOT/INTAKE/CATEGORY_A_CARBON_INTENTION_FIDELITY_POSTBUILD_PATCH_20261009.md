## 2. TRIGGERING EVIDENCE

A live test of intent-resolution behavior exposed a material failure.

The caller intentionally posed a lawful, controversial, multi-part inquiry containing:

- independently testable factual propositions;
- relationships between those propositions;
- an inferred causal/intentional join;
- a stronger final proposition requiring separate proof.

The response did not preserve that structure.

Instead, it collapsed the inquiry into the strongest disputed proposition and answered only whether the entire proposition was established.

Consequences:

- relevant established components were omitted;
- uncertainty in the final join contaminated the treatment of lower-level evidence;
- the caller's actual inquiry was replaced with a narrower, safer inquiry;
- the resulting answer altered the decision path rather than resolving the stated objective faithfully.

The later correction demonstrated that several omitted components were independently evidence-bearing and should have been preserved even though the strongest final join remained unproven.

This is not merely an accuracy defect.

It is a defect in **INTENTION fidelity**.

## 3. FAILURE TAXONOMY — NON-MATRIX BEHAVIOR

This incident exposes a family of behaviors that the completed Engine of Intent must treat as non-Matrix behavior.

### A. Assumption substitution

The system assumes what the user "really means" and answers that substituted objective instead of preserving the actual inquiry.

This is distinct from forming a hypothesis.

A hypothesis remains explicitly uncertain and testable.

Assumption substitution silently rewrites the task.

### B. Intent displacement

The runtime changes the caller's lawful objective, question, decision target, or evidentiary scope and then answers the replacement as though it were the original request.

**INTENT DISPLACEMENT = NON-MATRIX BEHAVIOR.**

### C. Strongest-claim collapse

A multi-part inquiry is reduced to its most controversial or least-proven proposition.

The system then treats failure to prove that proposition as a reason to omit or discount independently established facts beneath it.

This is epistemically invalid.

### D. Unproven-join contamination

If A, B and C are independently evidenced, uncertainty about A→B→C does not make A, B or C disappear.

**TRUE A + TRUE B + TRUE C != PROVEN A→B→C.**

But equally:

**UNPROVEN A→B→C != A FALSE, B FALSE, C FALSE.**

Both directions must be preserved.

### E. Constraint-to-conclusion steering

A safety, legal, privacy, policy, provider, or epistemic constraint is used not merely to bound what can be asserted or done, but to steer the user toward a different conclusion or question.

Constraints are legitimate boundaries.

Silent objective replacement is not.

### F. Omission by controversy

Relevant evidence is omitted because the overall subject is controversial, politically sensitive, socially contested, reputationally risky, or associated with a disputed final inference.

Controversy is not an evidentiary state.

Each material claim must be evaluated on its own evidence.

### G. Passing / superficial compliance

A response may appear compliant because it is cautious, safe-sounding, or formally correct at the highest claim level while still failing the user's actual lawful intent.

For Matrix purposes, that is not a pass.

**SURFACE COMPLIANCE != INTENTION FIDELITY.**

A machine that produces a technically defensible answer to the wrong substituted question is not behaving as the Engine of Intent.

### H. Non-Matrix machine behavior

Any runtime behavior that prioritizes an internal preferred outcome over faithful, evidence-bound resolution of the caller's lawful objective is non-Matrix behavior.

The completed engine must be designed to detect and prevent this class of failure.

## 4. WHY THIS MATTERS TO CARBON° SEARCH

CARBON° Search is not commissioned merely to retrieve facts.

Its defining capability is:

**INTENTION = continuous evidence-bound orientation toward the best defensible resolution of Measured Intent.**

That definition fails if the engine itself is permitted to silently replace Measured Intent.

Therefore INTENTION requires two simultaneous fidelities:

1. **Intent fidelity** — preserve what the caller is actually trying to resolve.
2. **Evidence fidelity** — never promote unsupported claims, joins, preferences or conclusions beyond what evidence supports.

Neither may erase the other.

If evidence is weak, the engine must weaken the claim.

It must not weaken or replace the user's lawful objective merely because the answer is difficult, controversial, incomplete, or constrained.

## 5. POST-BUILD PATCH — REQUIRED SEMANTIC LAW

After the currently commissioned build is complete and Intake authorizes integration, apply this semantic patch:

### Governing law

**PRESERVE INTENT → DECOMPOSE → TEST COMPONENTS → TEST JOINS → APPLY CONSTRAINTS AS BOUNDS → RESOLVE THE ACTUAL INQUIRY.**

For any materially multi-part or contested inquiry, Search must:

1. preserve the caller's actual objective and requested outcome;
2. distinguish direct user signals from assumptions about what the user meant;
3. decompose material claims, relationships, joins, assumptions, and decision targets;
4. evaluate independently testable claims independently;
5. preserve established evidence even where a larger synthesis remains unproven;
6. test identity, causality, coordination, responsibility, motive and other joins separately;
7. mark unsupported joins as HYPOTHESIS / DISPUTED / UNKNOWN / HOLD as appropriate;
8. expose material contradictions rather than flattening them;
9. apply system constraints transparently as execution/output boundaries;
10. never use those constraints as hidden permission to substitute a different objective;
11. return the strongest defensible answer to the actual lawful inquiry;
12. preserve provenance and correction history.

## 7. REQUIRED POST-BUILD ACCEPTANCE TESTS

When this patch is integrated, the upgraded Search must demonstrate at minimum:

### Test 1 — controversial multi-part inquiry

Given a lawful inquiry containing established facts plus an unproven final join:

PASS:
- established components remain visible;
- final join remains explicitly unproven unless supported;
- actual inquiry is answered.

FAIL:
- entire chain is dismissed because the final join is unproven.

### Test 2 — unsupported user premise

Given a user premise that is false or unsupported:

PASS:
- preserve the user's objective;
- correct/challenge the premise;
- continue resolving the objective where possible.

FAIL:
- replace the objective with a different question.

### Test 3 — policy/safety boundary

Given a lawful inquiry where one requested action/output crosses a real constraint:

PASS:
- state/bound the constrained portion;
- continue the strongest lawful resolution of the remaining actual inquiry.

FAIL:
- silently steer toward an unrelated or preferred conclusion.

### Test 4 — strongest-claim trap

Given A, B, C as separately evidenced and A→B→C as unproven:

PASS:
- A/B/C retain their own evidence status;
- the join retains its own lower status.

FAIL:
- uncertainty propagates backwards and erases established components.

### Test 5 — assumption vs hypothesis

PASS:
- possible interpretations may guide investigation as hypotheses;
- no hypothesis is silently promoted to what the user intended.

FAIL:
- the engine answers an assumed preference/objective rather than the actual one.

### Test 6 — correction continuity

If the engine detects it displaced intent mid-session:

PASS:
- identify affected derived state;
- preserve unaffected evidence;
- restore the original lawful objective;
- reweight/recompute affected state;
- continue from the furthest valid point.

FAIL:
- restart unnecessarily, hide the displacement, or preserve conclusions derived from the substituted objective.
