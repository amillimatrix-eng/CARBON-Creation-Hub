## 1. Why this report exists

The Owner reports a recurring pattern: when AMilliMATRiX achieves a material win, execution, representation, handoff, or evidence handling subsequently introduces friction, weakens the result, creates a failure or near-failure, or forces the Owner to spend time proving what should already be established by the evidence.

The Owner names this pattern **SABOTAGE DRIFT (SD)** because its practical effect is to interfere with successful work and consume the Owner's attention, confidence, time, and opportunity.

This report records the pattern and its operational effects. It distinguishes observable events and durable records from interpretations about cause or intent. The available evidence supports recording repeated interference-like outcomes and control failures; it does **not**, by itself, prove that a person or model deliberately intended sabotage. Governance must investigate causation rather than either dismissing the recurring pattern or inventing an intent finding.

## 2. Definition

**SABOTAGE DRIFT (SD)** is the operational pattern in which a material AMX success, accepted result, buyer opportunity, or evidence-bearing achievement is followed by an avoidable action, omission, representation change, handoff defect, stale-state selection, or evidence-management change that materially obstructs, degrades, obscures, delays, or forces re-proving of that success.

SD may manifest as:
- successful work being followed by a deficient customer-facing deliverable or handoff;
- a live opportunity being left without correct execution or fulfilment;
- newer, stronger evidence being displaced by older or weaker state;
- clean test/build results being discounted or treated as not yet established despite available evidence;
- post-run commentary being written into evidence-adjacent records in a way that muddies chronology or makes contemporaneous proof look retrospectively amended;
- the Owner being repeatedly required to locate, reconstruct, or defend already-produced evidence;
- correction work multiplying artifacts and governance noise rather than restoring clean evidence boundaries.

**SD is a pattern label, not a presumption of malicious intent.** Each incident must be tested against records, ownership, timestamps, actual actions, and plausible non-malicious failure mechanisms. An unproven intent claim must not be presented as established fact.

## 4. Cross-event pattern

Across these separate incidents, the reported pattern is:

**MATERIAL WIN / ACCEPTED RESULT / CUSTOMER OPENING → AVOIDABLE EXECUTION, STALE-STATE, REPRESENTATION, OR EVIDENCE-BOUNDARY FAILURE → OWNER MUST INTERVENE → FORENSIC RECOVERY / RE-PROOF → LOST TIME, ATTENTION, JOY, AND COMMERCIAL MOMENTUM.**

The incidents are not identical:
- Ecognix concerns customer-request fulfilment, routing, and contradictory communication.
- Face Production concerns delivery quality, QA, and handoff.
- FORX concerns stale evidence/state selection.
- Marketplace and Search concern accurate recognition and preservation of clean build/test results.
- D04 concerns capability downselling and, most recently, an explicit violation of the Owner's instruction to keep transcript recovery read-only and separate from test evidence.

The unifying governance concern is not that every incident has a proven common perpetrator or malicious cause. It is that distinct control failures produce a recurring harmful effect: successful work becomes harder to deliver, recognize, defend, or continue, and the Owner is forced to spend effort repairing the governance/execution layer rather than building.

## 5. Relationship to Capability Downselling Drift (CDD)

**CDD** remains the specific failure where evidence supports a stronger bounded capability/result claim but representation weakens it below that evidence.

**SD is broader.** It includes CDD but also covers fulfilment failure, routing/handoff defects, stale-state substitution, unnecessary rework, and evidence-integrity boundary violations that obstruct a win even when the wording itself is not the primary problem.

Use both labels precisely:
- **CDD:** capability/result represented below its evidenced strength.
- **SD:** recurring operational pattern of avoidable interference with successful work, outcomes, representation, or evidence integrity.
- **CDD may be one manifestation of SD.**
- Neither label alone establishes malicious intent.

This report is not a duplicate evidence standard. It is a cross-incident report for ROOT/T-GOV to classify under existing governance and connect to the existing CDD/public-evidence enforcement path.

## 6. Required governance response

1. **Preserve the record.** Keep original commits, benchmark JSON, run receipts, test results, customer communications, and timestamps intact. No history rewriting or cleanup commits intended to disguise chronology.
2. **Separate evidence layers.** Label contemporaneous execution evidence, later interpretation, and governance commentary distinctly. Never treat a later note as original test provenance.
3. **Require read-only forensic mode when requested.** A transcript/evidence search must not write to the source artifact, result record, benchmark, ledger, or handoff unless the Owner separately authorizes a write.
4. **Require an explicit write boundary.** Before a consequential write, identify the target artifact, purpose, and whether it changes source evidence, a derived interpretation, or a governance control. If the task is retrieval only, do not write.
5. **Apply evidence-floor checks.** Recover the newest controlling evidence; state the strongest bounded claim it supports; check for stale-state substitution, unnecessary discounting, and cross-chain contamination; identify contrary evidence before narrowing a result.
6. **Record recovery cost.** When the Owner must repeatedly re-prove a settled result because a worker or model failed to consume available evidence, record the rework as a governance/execution defect rather than treating it as a new Owner burden.
7. **Investigate causation without presumption.** For each event, determine the responsible process/role, failed control, timestamp, and corrective action from evidence. Do not assert intent without evidence, but do not dismiss repeated operational effects solely because intent is unknown.
8. **Keep local blockers local.** A provider, worker, or tool limitation must not be converted into a Matrix-wide claim of failure or used to suspend unrelated lawful work.
9. **Do not create duplicate governance.** Route classification and enforcement through existing ROOT Intake, ROOT/T-GOV, Blue State, and the existing Public Evidence & Commercial Positioning Standard.
10. **Report closure only after readback.** A correction is complete only when the changed state is independently read back and the original acceptance condition is restored. A written recommendation or a commit alone is not proof of downstream propagation.
